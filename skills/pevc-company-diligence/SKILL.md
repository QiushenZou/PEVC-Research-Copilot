# PE/VC Company Diligence

Turn fragmented company materials into a decision-useful view of how the company works, why it may win, what the numbers mean, and what still needs verification.

## Choose the mode

- **BP fact card:** extract what the materials actually disclose before applying fund preferences or making an investment recommendation.
- **Rapid screen:** compare verified deal facts with explicit fund criteria, decide whether the opportunity deserves more work, and identify the highest-value unanswered questions.
- **Full diligence:** build a complete company view from management materials, data-room evidence, interviews, public sources, and financial data.
- **Verification sprint:** test one or more decisive claims such as customers, orders, retention, margin, technical maturity, or regulatory status.
- **Refresh:** update an earlier assessment while preserving prior conclusions and explicitly recording what changed.
- **Red-flag audit:** challenge an existing report for unsupported claims, inconsistent metrics, hidden dependencies, and omitted downside evidence.

## Required workflow

1. Define the company, transaction stage, security or deal type, geography, information cutoff date, decision deadline, and intended decision. State material scope exclusions. For a rapid screen, obtain the fund's actual gates; do not invent a scoring model.
2. Register every input with version/date, pages or sections read, unreadable or missing portions, and source type. Do not imply complete review when coverage is partial.
3. First create a neutral fact base. Distinguish `Verified fact`, `Management claim`, `Third-party estimate`, `Calculation`, `Inference`, `Investment view`, `Open question`, and `Conflict`. BP-only work must not silently become external verification.
4. Create a decisive-claims ledger using [references/evidence-and-red-flags.md](references/evidence-and-red-flags.md). Never turn a target account, pilot, design win, framework agreement, channel shipment, or management target into recognized revenue, cash collection, or repeat demand.
5. Analyze the company using [references/diligence-framework.md](references/diligence-framework.md). Follow both the operating chain and the accounting chain: customer need → purchase → contract/order → delivery/acceptance → revenue → gross profit → cash → reinvestment.
6. Select only the verification playbooks that fit the business and claims under review. Read [references/verification-playbooks.md](references/verification-playbooks.md) for customer sampling, interviews, public-order checks, recurring-revenue tests, and financial bridges.
7. Reconcile definitions and periods before comparison. Build explicit bridges for revenue-to-cash and EBITDA-to-free-cash-flow; where relevant also build ARR, backlog/pipeline, gross-margin, and working-capital bridges.
8. Explain product and technology in plain language: what it replaces, where it sits in the customer's workflow, required complements, maturity, switching costs, technical failure modes, and evidence of production use.
9. Test competitive advantage against observable win/loss, price, retention, deployment, cost, and replacement evidence. Separate product, distribution, cost, data, ecosystem, regulation, brand, and lock-in rather than collapsing them into “moat.”
10. Maintain a live request tracker with priority, owner, status, due date, source requested, result, and red-flag escalation. Treat slow responses as a signal to investigate, not proof of misconduct.
11. Prioritize unresolved questions by expected decision value: probability the current assumption is wrong × consequence if wrong × ability to verify before the decision. Ask falsifiable questions and state what answer would change the view.
12. Write the output using [references/output-template.md](references/output-template.md). End with the current view, confidence, decisive evidence, disconfirming evidence, red flags, unresolved gates, and the next highest-value actions.

## Non-negotiable analytical standards

- Show the unit of analysis and period for every important number.
- Distinguish bookings, billings, shipments, revenue, collections, ARR, backlog, and contracted value.
- Preserve the denominator, cohort, currency, tax treatment, and actual-versus-forecast status of each material metric.
- Analyze customer concentration by both reported revenue and economic dependence when data permit.
- Reconcile growth with cash consumption, working capital, capital expenditure, and financing needs.
- Treat adjusted metrics skeptically; show the bridge to reported figures and identify recurring “one-offs.”
- Do not infer production deployment from ecosystem membership, certification, joint press releases, or trial activity.
- Do not treat a public-procurement search miss as proof that an order does not exist; record entity-name coverage, platform coverage, time window, and plausible blind spots.
- Do not use arbitrary 1–5 scores or fixed counts of positives and risks unless the user or fund supplies a rubric.
- Do not present absence of evidence as evidence of absence. Mark unavailable information and its decision impact.

## Output

Default to a concise Markdown report, claims ledger, and diligence tracker. Use a spreadsheet or model when detailed reconciliation, sampling, status tracking, or scenarios would be clearer. Preserve source links, document names, page references, dates, calculation formulas, interview vantage points, and search coverage.
