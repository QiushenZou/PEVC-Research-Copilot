# Valuation and return analysis

The purpose is not to display every valuation method. It is to show what must happen for the proposed security, price, ownership, and financing structure to produce an acceptable fund outcome—and how the outcome fails.

## Scale by decision stage

- **Screening:** use a transparent feasibility range. State missing terms and the ownership, earnings, or exit requirement that must eventually be true. Do not fabricate a full model.
- **Preliminary IC:** show preliminary entry assumptions, underwriting/downside ranges, and the sensitivities that should drive diligence or negotiations.
- **Final IC:** reconcile the executable terms and capitalization to the model; show coherent scenarios, sensitivities, liquidity or financing risks, and arithmetic tie-outs.
- **Update:** bridge prior to current entry terms, operating assumptions, capital needs, exit assumptions, and investor returns. Explain every material movement.

## Shared requirements

- State valuation date, currency, security, fully diluted share count, debt, cash, transaction fees, and treatment of options, warrants, convertibles, and other claims.
- Reconcile enterprise value to equity value and pre-money to post-money where relevant.
- Show the operating metric, definition, period, and source used for every multiple.
- Adjust comparable companies and transactions for business mix, growth, margin, scale, geography, control, liquidity, security rights, and market date.
- Separate management case, underwriting case, and sensitivities.
- Connect each scenario to an operating narrative, cash/financing path, capitalization path, and plausible exit mechanism.
- Show fund-level relevance when available: expected proceeds, gross return, ownership at exit, share of fund, and capital reserved or at risk.
- Do not assign probabilities without a defensible basis. An unweighted range is better than invented precision.
- State the valuation or terms at which the recommendation changes.

## PE/control analysis

At final IC, normally show:

1. reported-to-normalized EBITDA or other earnings bridge, with source and treatment of each adjustment;
2. entry enterprise value, equity value, multiple, debt, cash, fees, rollover, management equity, and sources/uses;
3. operating drivers for revenue, margin, working capital, maintenance/growth capex, tax, interest, and cash conversion;
4. debt tranches, amortization, cash sweep, refinancing, covenant tests, minimum liquidity, and sponsor equity requirements;
5. exit year, exit metric, exit multiple, net debt, equity value, investor proceeds, MOIC, and IRR;
6. value-creation attribution across operating growth, margin, M&A, deleveraging, and multiple movement;
7. sensitivities across operating performance, leverage, liquidity, exit timing, and exit multiple.

The downside must test covenant and liquidity survival, refinancing access, and additional-equity needs—not merely reduce the exit multiple. Do not count a management plan, leverage, and multiple expansion as three independent sources of protection.

## VC/minority growth analysis

At final IC, normally show:

1. pre-money, primary and secondary proceeds, post-money, option pool treatment, security, and fully diluted ownership;
2. liquidation preferences, participation, conversion, seniority, anti-dilution, dividends, warrants, redemption or other economic rights where relevant;
3. cash needed to reach the next value-inflecting milestone and evidence supporting the milestone budget;
4. burn, runway, future rounds, valuation assumptions, dilution, pro rata participation, and follow-on reserves;
5. exit timing and equity-value scenarios tied to revenue/earnings/strategic milestones and a plausible buyer or public-market logic;
6. waterfall-aware investor proceeds, MOIC, IRR, and contribution to fund return;
7. sensitivities to growth, retention or repeat behavior, gross margin, burn, financing timing, dilution, exit value, and ownership maintenance.

Answer both: **what must the company become, and how much of that outcome will the fund still own?** A large exit that returns immaterial capital to the fund is not automatically an attractive investment.

## Scenario discipline

For each scenario state:

- demand, customer adoption, retention/repeat behavior, and pricing;
- gross margin and operating expense;
- working capital, capex, cash burn, or debt path;
- financing availability, cost, dilution, or sponsor support;
- exit timing, metric, multiple, buyer/public-market rationale, and transaction friction.

Do not combine upside revenue with downside costs, or downside operations with frictionless refinancing/fundraising, unless explicitly justified.

## Sensitivity and decision thresholds

Prefer variables that can change the recommendation. Examples:

- maximum entry price at target return;
- minimum EBITDA/cash conversion needed to avoid liquidity stress;
- minimum growth or retention needed to support the exit case;
- maximum future dilution or follow-on capital before fund returns fall below threshold;
- exit timing or multiple at which base-case returns become unacceptable.

Turn thresholds into diligence tests, negotiation positions, reserves, covenants, or approval conditions.

## Arithmetic and model checks

Before delivery:

- sources equal uses;
- EV/equity and pre/post-money bridges reconcile;
- ownership percentages and option pools sum correctly;
- cash flow drives debt paydown or cash balance without unexplained plugs;
- exit equity value flows through the correct security waterfall;
- MOIC and IRR use consistent dates and cash flows;
- memo tables match the cited model version;
- update bridges reproduce both prior and current reported outputs.

If the model is unavailable or fails a tie-out, mark returns provisional and explain the decision consequence.
