# Decision-stage playbooks

Choose one stage. Scale evidence and detail to the actual approval gate rather than treating every memo as a final investment recommendation.

## Routing matrix

| Stage | Decision being requested | Minimum decision evidence | Appropriate output | Do not pretend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Screening | Spend more time, seek access, or decline | Fund fit, problem/product, initial market and company signal, obvious conflicts/red flags, plausible return path, critical tests | 1–3 page gate memo | That preliminary terms, management claims, TAM, or early logos are fully diligenced |
| Preliminary IC | Authorize diligence, indication/LOI, exclusivity work, or resource allocation | Testable thesis, early customer/market work, historical financial snapshot, preliminary valuation/terms, downside framing, diligence plan | 4–10 page preliminary memo | That final legal, QoE, technical, customer, financing, or reference work is complete |
| Final IC | Approve investment and closing authority | Reconciled diligence, executable terms, underwriting case, returns and sensitivities, residual risks, closing conditions, ownership of post-close actions | Full decision memo with appendices | That a mitigation is effective unless funded, controlled, and enforceable |
| Update/addendum | Reconfirm, amend, reprice, pause, waive, or reverse a prior decision | Prior decision baseline, change log, new evidence, model/terms delta, condition status, revised recommendation | Delta memo; reproduce only changed or decision-critical sections | That silence means an old condition was satisfied |

## Screening memo

### Required opening

- **Ask:** advance, hold, or decline.
- **Reason:** the strongest positive signal, strongest disconfirming signal, and the one uncertainty that dominates the next decision.
- **Cost of next step:** information/access needed, expected time or spend, and stop condition.

### Minimum analysis

1. Fund mandate and check-size fit.
2. One-sentence company and customer problem definition.
3. Evidence of demand or adoption, explicitly tagged by status.
4. Why the opportunity could produce a fund-relevant outcome.
5. Three to five fatal or gating questions and the cheapest credible test for each.
6. Initial conflicts, reputational issues, legal/regulatory flags, or structural deal blockers visible from available evidence.

For PE, test whether earnings quality and financing could plausibly support the transaction. For VC, test whether the team, wedge, early adoption, and ownership path could plausibly support venture-scale returns.

## Preliminary IC

### Required opening

- State the specific authority requested: diligence budget, indication, LOI, exclusivity, management time, or another gate.
- Separate what has been verified from what remains a management case.
- State the preliminary valuation range or explain why price is not yet decisionable.

### Minimum analysis

1. Three to five thesis propositions with supporting and contrary evidence.
2. Initial customer, market, competitor, product, team, and economics findings.
3. Historical performance sufficient to identify the main operating drivers and data-quality issues.
4. Preliminary deal and capitalization analysis.
5. Base/downside return range using only defensible assumptions.
6. Diligence plan ranked by decision impact, with owner, method, evidence required, and consequence.
7. Conditions for returning to final IC and explicit walk-away or repricing triggers.

## Final IC

### Required opening

- Exact investment approval, signing/closing authority, check size, security, valuation, ownership, leverage or reserve, and approval conditions.
- Underwriting-case return and downside outcome.
- Residual risks the fund is knowingly accepting.

### Minimum analysis

1. Reconciled sources-and-uses or round composition, capitalization, economic and governance terms.
2. Diligence conclusions by workstream, including adverse and inconclusive findings.
3. Bridge from reported/management results to the underwriting case.
4. Coherent upside, base, and downside operating narratives connected to cash, financing, dilution/leverage, and exit.
5. Returns and sensitivities that tie arithmetically to the model.
6. Risks translated into transaction, price, covenant, governance, reserve, insurance/indemnity, or monitoring actions.
7. Closing conditions, required documents, post-close milestones, owners, deadlines, and escalation triggers.

If key evidence remains missing, use `approve with conditions` only when the condition is specific, satisfiable before the fund becomes irreversibly exposed, and has a defined failure consequence. Otherwise hold or decline.

## Update or addendum

Start with a delta table:

| Item | Prior memo/decision | New evidence or event | Impact on thesis/terms/returns/risk | Status | Action requested |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then address only:

1. reason for the update and information cutoff;
2. status of each prior approval condition;
3. model and valuation bridge from old to new;
4. newly supported or falsified thesis propositions;
5. new risks and changed mitigations;
6. revised recommendation and authority requested.

Classify prior conditions as `closed`, `open`, `waived`, or `breached`. Identify scope drift: a revised investment should not inherit approval merely because an earlier, materially different transaction was approved.

## Challenge review

A challenge review is an overlay, not a decision stage. Apply it to any memo when asked to red-team, prepare IC questions, or audit the logic. Preserve the original recommendation, then show:

- strongest case against it;
- claims resting on weak or circular evidence;
- omitted base rates and correlated risks;
- scenario or capitalization errors;
- terms at which the recommendation would change;
- evidence that would resolve the disagreement.
