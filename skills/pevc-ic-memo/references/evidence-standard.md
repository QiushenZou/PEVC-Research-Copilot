# Evidence standard

Use this standard for decision-driving claims, not for every descriptive sentence.

## Claim ledger

| Claim | Status | Support | Counterevidence/conflict | Source and date | Confidence | Decision implication | Remaining test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Allowed status labels:

- **Verified fact:** supported by a reliable primary record or independently corroborated evidence.
- **Management claim:** supplied by the company or its advisers and not independently verified.
- **Third-party estimate:** external estimate whose method, date, and scope must be stated.
- **Analyst calculation:** reproducible calculation from cited inputs.
- **Inference:** conclusion drawn from stated facts; explain the reasoning.
- **Investment view:** judgment about attractiveness, durability, or risk; do not present it as fact.
- **Open question:** unresolved matter with an owner and decision consequence.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer evidence closest to the underlying event:

1. executed agreements, bank/tax records, audited statements, system exports, regulator filings, customer or supplier confirmations;
2. signed internal records, board materials, detailed operational datasets, direct interviews with identified counterparties;
3. management presentations, adviser materials, data-room summaries, draft agreements;
4. reputable third-party research and databases with visible method and date;
5. news, marketing materials, unsourced databases, anecdotes, and search snippets.

Source rank is not proof by itself. Test scope, date, completeness, independence, and incentives. A primary source can still be stale, selective, or internally inconsistent.

## Citation standard

For material claims, cite enough for a reviewer to reproduce the finding:

`[Document or dataset title, issuer/owner, date or period, page/section/cell/query, file or model version]`

For interviews include interviewee role, date, interviewer, and whether the statement was independently corroborated. For web evidence preserve the direct URL and access date. For calculations cite the inputs and formula or model tab.

## Conflict handling

When sources disagree:

1. normalize definitions, periods, entities, currency, gross/net presentation, and cut-off date;
2. identify whether the conflict is timing, scope, method, error, or incentive driven;
3. use the most decision-relevant evidence only with a stated reason;
4. retain the alternative value and show its effect if material;
5. escalate unresolved conflicts into a test, condition, repricing, or stop decision.

Never average conflicting values merely to create a number.

## Confidence and materiality

Use `high`, `medium`, or `low` confidence only after considering source quality, corroboration, recency, and completeness. Confidence describes the evidence, not enthusiasm for the deal.

A claim is decision-critical if changing it could alter any of:

- advance/decline or approve/pass;
- price, check size, ownership, leverage, reserve, or follow-on decision;
- security, covenant, preference, governance, indemnity, or closing condition;
- return threshold, liquidity survival, or fund-level relevance.

Decision-critical low-confidence claims belong in the opening section, not only in an appendix.

## Evidence request format

| Priority | Claim to verify | Evidence required | Test method | Owner | Deadline | Decision consequence if unresolved/adverse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Prioritize by decision impact and irreversibility, not by ease of collection.
