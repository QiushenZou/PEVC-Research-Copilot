# Decision-quality review

Run after drafting and before circulation. Review the decision, evidence, economics, downside, and actionability—not just writing quality.

## One-minute test

Can a committee member identify within one minute:

- exact decision and authority requested;
- memo stage and information cutoff;
- price/security/check size and ownership or leverage;
- underwriting return range and principal downside, if decisionable;
- three strongest thesis claims and their evidence status;
- three decision-critical unknowns;
- approval conditions and failure consequences?

If not, revise the opening page.

## Stage fitness

- **Screening:** does the memo identify the cheapest decisive next tests and avoid pretending diligence is complete?
- **Preliminary:** is the requested authorization explicit, and are unverified assumptions tied to a diligence plan and walk-away/repricing triggers?
- **Final:** are terms executable, findings reconciled, returns tied, residual risks accepted explicitly, and conditions enforceable before irreversible exposure?
- **Update:** is the prior decision preserved, every material delta bridged, and each prior condition marked closed, open, waived, or breached?

## Thesis test

For every proposition ask:

- Is it independently testable rather than a restatement of another claim?
- Is the evidence about this company, customer, or transaction—not merely the market?
- What reliable evidence supports and contradicts it?
- What would falsify it?
- Is the claimed advantage already reflected in price or terms?
- Which metric will reveal deterioration before the financial statements do?
- What action follows if the proposition weakens?

## Evidence audit

- Can verified facts, management claims, estimates, calculations, inferences, views, and open questions be distinguished?
- Are decision-driving claims traceable to document/date/page, dataset/query, interview, or model version?
- Were conflicting definitions, periods, entities, and figures reconciled or displayed?
- Are low-confidence but high-impact claims visible in the main memo?
- Has absence of evidence been mistaken for evidence of absence?
- Does the confidence label reflect corroboration, recency, completeness, and source incentives?

## Bias checks

- **Confirmation bias:** was contrary evidence actively sought and given a fair decision implication?
- **Base-rate neglect:** how do comparable companies, financings, integrations, restructurings, or exits usually perform?
- **TAM substitution:** is market size replacing evidence of access, win rate, adoption, or economics?
- **Narrative-number disconnect:** do operating assumptions produce the stated financial outcome?
- **Price blindness:** would the recommendation change at a materially different price or structure?
- **Sunk-cost bias:** is prior diligence spend or partner sponsorship affecting the conclusion?
- **Founder/management halo:** is team quality supported by execution, references, recruiting, and learning evidence?
- **Pseudo-diversification:** are customers, suppliers, products, channels, or risks economically correlated despite different labels?
- **Mitigation theater:** are actions called mitigations even though they are unfunded, uncontrolled, late, or unenforceable?

## PE/control checks

- Are EBITDA adjustments evidenced, recurring costs restored, and one-time benefits removed?
- Do working capital, maintenance capex, tax, interest, and cash leakage reconcile to cash conversion?
- Does downside test liquidity, covenants, refinancing, sponsor support, and recovery?
- Is value creation separated from leverage and multiple expansion?
- Are management rollover, incentives, governance, and succession consistent with the plan?

## VC/minority checks

- Is product-market fit supported by behavior, retention/repeat use, willingness to pay, or references appropriate to stage?
- Is growth separated from subsidized acquisition, channel concentration, or one-off contracts?
- Are runway, next-round dependency, dilution, preference stack, pro rata needs, and follow-on reserves modeled?
- Is the required exit value plausible, and will the fund own enough at exit for the outcome to matter?
- Are founder/team strengths and gaps tied to the next milestones rather than pedigree?

## Downside consistency

- Does downside include weaker demand, slower collections, margin pressure, delayed execution, and financing friction where relevant?
- Does it include the cash required to reach exit, recovery, or the next round?
- Are debt covenants, preference stacks, dilution, senior claims, and transaction costs reflected?
- Are correlations between risks recognized?
- Are mitigants within investor control, contractually enforceable, funded, and timely?
- Is there an observable trigger for stop, reprice, restructure, reduce size, reserve more capital, or reverse approval?

## Recommendation integrity

The recommendation should follow from evidence and fund economics. Check that:

- the strongest contrary case is answered rather than omitted;
- unresolved items are compatible with the recommended stage;
- conditions are specific, measurable, owned, time-bound, and linked to a failure consequence;
- the recommendation changes when decisive assumptions cross the stated thresholds;
- the main memo and model use the same terms, assumptions, and version;
- no decision-critical uncertainty has been hidden in an appendix.
